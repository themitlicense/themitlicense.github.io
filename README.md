# The MIT License — Maintainer Manifesto

### Free Code. Not Free Labor.

**You can use the code. You cannot claim the developer's time.**

The MIT License gives people broad freedom to use, modify, distribute, and sell software. We support that freedom. But receiving permission to use code does not automatically entitle anyone to free consulting, urgent bug fixes, dedicated support, or guaranteed maintenance from the people who built it.

**The MIT License — Maintainer Manifesto** is an independent, voluntary initiative calling for more respectful and reciprocal behavior around MIT-licensed open-source projects.

> **A license to use the code is not a license to demand the author's time.**

## What we stand for

- **Keep the freedom.** The MIT License remains exactly what it is. Commercial use, private modifications, and redistribution are legitimate.
- **Respect maintainers' time.** Issues are requests, not support contracts. Help may be offered freely, but it is not automatically owed.
- **Give credit accurately.** Preserve the copyright and permission notices required by MIT; recognize original creators publicly when appropriate.
- **Contribute when you can.** Useful bug reports, documentation, testing, code, sponsorship, and thoughtful feedback can all help. None is a condition of MIT use.
- **Make support expectations explicit.** Free software and paid services can coexist without contradiction.
- **Treat people as people.** Neither maintainers nor users deserve harassment, demands, or public shaming.

**We are not asking anyone to give up their MIT rights.** We are asking people not to confuse those rights with an obligation for someone else to work for free.

## Adopt the manifesto

If this message represents how you maintain an MIT-licensed project, you can voluntarily display a badge in your README.

```md
[![MIT — Free Code, Not Free Labor. Independent manifesto](https://img.shields.io/badge/MIT-Free_Code_Not_Free_Labor-FF6B35?style=flat-square)](https://github.com/themitlicense/themitlicense.github.io)
```

Or use a plain-language statement:

```md
### Open-source expectations

This software is available under the license stated in this repository.
That license does not, by itself, include guaranteed support, maintenance,
customization, or consulting. Constructive reports and contributions are welcome.

We support the independent [Free Code. Not Free Labor. manifesto](https://github.com/themitlicense/themitlicense.github.io).
```

Adoption is **optional, free, reversible, and requires no registration**. The badge expresses support for a cultural statement; it is **not** an MIT license badge, a certification, or a modification to the software's license. Where support is separately promised by a contract or another commitment, those obligations still matter.

For additional variants, including a badge that does not mention MIT, see [the adoption kit](adoption-kit.md).

## What this initiative is not

- **Not a new or modified MIT License.** We do not add conditions to third-party MIT-licensed software.
- **Not an official MIT publication.** We are not affiliated with or endorsed by the Massachusetts Institute of Technology.
- **Not a certification body.** Displaying the badge does not certify licensing compliance, project quality, or individual behavior.
- **Not an anti-business campaign.** Commercial use of MIT-licensed software is permitted. Respectful reciprocity is encouraged, not legally demanded by MIT.
- **Not a guarantee of support.** Contributions, reviews, and responses in this project are voluntary, too.

For the actual legal terms of the MIT License, consult the [Open Source Initiative's MIT License text](https://opensource.org/license/mit).
