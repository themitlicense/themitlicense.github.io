## A2. Homepage — full copy

**Eyebrow:** AN INDEPENDENT OPEN-SOURCE MANIFESTO

# FREE CODE.
# NOT FREE LABOR.

**You can use the code. You can't claim the developer's time.**

The MIT License gives you broad freedom to use, modify, distribute and sell software. That's a feature, not a loophole.

But that freedom doesn't automatically include free consulting, urgent bug fixes, dedicated support or guaranteed maintenance from the person who wrote it.

**We believe in open-source freedom — and in respecting the humans who make it possible.**

**Primary CTA:** Add to your README  
**Secondary CTA:** Read the manifesto

**Trust line:** Voluntary principles. No new license restrictions. No registration.

### Section: The misunderstanding

**The code was shared. The maintainer wasn't hired.**

An issue is a request, not a support contract. A pull request is a contribution, not a demand. Using an open-source project is not an invitation to schedule the maintainer's evenings, weekends or priorities.

You may ask. Maintainers may say no. Both things can be true.

### Section: What we're asking for

**Not permission. Not payment. Respect.**

Keep the notices required by the license. Report problems helpfully. Give credit when you can. Share improvements when you choose. If a project creates commercial value for you, consider how you might return some value.

None of this is an extra condition on MIT-licensed software. It's a better way to treat people.

### Section: Why adopt this manifesto?

**A short statement can set a useful boundary.**

If you maintain an MIT-licensed project, add a small badge or a plain-language note to your README. It communicates that the software may be used under its license while support, customization and your time remain separate decisions.

No signup. No approval. No pledge to provide anything. Just a clear signal.

**CTA:** Add to your README

### Section: Clear boundaries

**What it is:** an independent, voluntary manifesto about respectful open-source collaboration.  
**What it isn't:** an official MIT publication, a new software license, or a requirement for using MIT-licensed software.

**CTA:** Read the FAQ

**Fork the code. Not the person.**

Share the idea if it represents you. Or don't. The right to choose is part of what we defend.

## A5. FAQ — publishable answers

### Is this the official MIT License website?

No. This is an **independent manifesto** about respect for open-source maintainers, founded by Antonio Trento. It is not affiliated with or endorsed by the Massachusetts Institute of Technology. For the MIT License text, see the [Open Source Initiative](https://opensource.org/license/mit).

### Does adding the badge modify the MIT License?

No. It expresses an ethical position only. It adds no legal restrictions to any MIT-licensed software, and it must not be inserted as a new clause in a project's `LICENSE` file.

### Does the MIT License require me to contribute back?

No. MIT-licensed software may generally be used, modified and distributed, including commercially, subject to the license conditions. Sharing fixes, documentation or tests is encouraged here, not required.

### Does the MIT License require giving credit?

It requires preservation of the copyright and permission notices in copies or substantial portions of the software. Public acknowledgment in a README, blog post or product page is a separate courtesy encouraged by this manifesto, not an additional MIT requirement.

### Can I use MIT-licensed software in a commercial product?

Yes, under the license's terms. Commercial use is not unethical by itself. If someone's work materially benefits your business, contributing back may be a constructive choice, not a condition.

### Can I request help from the author?

Of course. Asking respectfully is fine. The MIT License does not itself obligate an author to provide support, response times, customization or consulting. A separate service agreement may create its own commitments.

### Is reporting a bug an unreasonable request?

No. Useful bug reports and constructive criticism are valuable. The problem is treating unpaid maintainers as if they had agreed to provide guaranteed service.

### Are maintainers free to ignore every issue?

Under the MIT License alone, no support obligation is created. A maintainer may still have responsibilities from other agreements or roles. The manifesto encourages clear expectations, not contempt for users or contributors.

### Is this a campaign against companies that use open source?

No. Companies contribute enormously to open source, and commercial use is legitimate. This project challenges entitlement and poor collaboration, whether they come from companies or individuals.

### Do I need to register or pay to adopt the manifesto?

No. Simply add the badge or a statement in your own README. You can remove it whenever you like.

### Are listed adopters verified or certified?

There is no certification. If an adopters showcase is introduced, it should display only public, verifiable uses with appropriate permission. Inclusion would not endorse a project's quality or behavior.

### Why is the initiative linked to Antonio Trento?

Antonio Trento created the initiative and is credited openly. The manifesto is a standalone contribution to the open-source community, not a requirement to visit or purchase anything from the founder.

### Can I translate or adapt the manifesto?

We welcome translation proposals and responsible adaptation, subject to the permissions published for the text and assets. Translations must preserve the distinction between legal MIT requirements and voluntary principles. Until licensing terms for these original materials are formally published, do not assume unrestricted reuse beyond linking.

## A6. About — full copy

# Why this exists

**Open source grants freedoms. It doesn't erase the value of the people behind the work.**

The MIT License is intentionally permissive. That freedom is worth protecting. This project started from a simple frustration: people sometimes accept the freedom to use software, then assume the author must also provide unlimited free time, technical support and maintenance.

The answer isn't to rewrite the license. It's to improve the expectations around it.

**The MIT License — Maintainer Manifesto** is an independent, voluntary statement about attribution, constructive contributions, realistic support expectations and respect for maintainers.

## B5. FAQ — risposte italiane

### È il sito ufficiale della licenza MIT?

No. È un manifesto indipendente ideato da Antonio Trento. Non rappresenta né è approvato dal Massachusetts Institute of Technology. Per il testo autentico della licenza consulta l'[Open Source Initiative](https://opensource.org/license/mit).

### Aggiungere il badge modifica la licenza MIT?

No. È una dichiarazione etica volontaria, non una condizione di licenza. Non va inserita come clausola nel testo `LICENSE`.

### La MIT obbliga a condividere le modifiche?

No. Il manifesto invita a contribuire quando possibile, ma non trasforma questo invito in un obbligo.

### La MIT obbliga a citare l'autore?

Impone di conservare gli avvisi di copyright e licenza secondo i suoi termini. Citare pubblicamente l'autore o il repository in un articolo o sito è un riconoscimento aggiuntivo consigliato, non un obbligo generale della MIT.

### Posso usare software MIT in un prodotto commerciale?

Sì, rispettando la licenza. Guadagnare con software open source non è di per sé scorretto. Può però essere utile contribuire al progetto da cui si trae valore.

### Posso chiedere aiuto all'autore?

Certamente. Chiedere non è pretendere. La MIT non prevede di per sé un'assistenza garantita; eventuali contratti di supporto sono separati.

### Segnalare un bug significa pretendere?

No. Segnalazioni precise e rispettose sono preziose. Il problema nasce dalle pretese di intervento gratuito e immediato.

### Il progetto è contro le aziende?

No. Anche le aziende contribuiscono all'open source. Il manifesto critica comportamenti scorretti, non il legittimo utilizzo commerciale.

### Serve registrarsi, pagare o firmare?

No. Basta aggiungere volontariamente il badge o il testo al README. Puoi rimuoverli in qualsiasi momento.

### Il badge certifica il repository?

No. Non è un riconoscimento ufficiale e non sostituisce l'indicazione della licenza effettiva.

### Perché compare Antonio Trento?

Perché è il fondatore dell'iniziativa. Il suo profilo professionale è indicato con trasparenza, ma aderire al manifesto non significa pubblicizzare il suo sito.

### Posso tradurre il manifesto?

Sono benvenute proposte di traduzione. I diritti sui contenuti originali devono essere indicati esplicitamente prima di autorizzare riusi estesi; la traduzione non deve introdurre nuovi obblighi MIT.
