# Contributing to The MIT License — Maintainer Manifesto

**Thank you for helping improve the conversation around open-source maintenance.**

This is an independent cultural initiative, **not a new software license** and not an official MIT project. Contributions should make its message more accurate, clearer, more inclusive, or more useful to maintainers and users.

> **Be kind. Be precise. Respect boundaries.**

## Before contributing

1. Read the [manifesto](foundation.md) and the [community guidelines](community.md).
2. Check existing issues and pull requests before opening another.
3. Remember the distinction: **MIT rights are legal; the manifesto's recommendations are voluntary.**
4. Keep submissions focused. Small, well-reasoned corrections are easier to review than broad rewrites.

## Contributions we welcome

- **Accuracy:** fixing legal ambiguities, incorrect claims, broken references, or misleading wording, with sources when relevant.
- **Editorial improvements:** improving clarity, brevity, tone, accessibility, or consistency with the manifesto.
- **Translations:** natural, locally appropriate translations and review by fluent speakers, while preserving the legal/ethical distinction.
- **Adoption materials:** clearer badges, README statements, and accessibility text that make the maintainer's benefit obvious.
- **Constructive criticism:** disagreement backed by reasoning, examples, and proposed improvements.
- **Evidence:** reliable references that help explain maintainer workload, expectations, and open-source collaboration.

We especially value feedback from maintainers who would **not** adopt the badge: understanding why is more useful than collecting superficial endorsements.

## How to propose a change

Use an issue to discuss a substantial editorial change, or a pull request for a straightforward correction.

A useful submission answers:

1. **What needs changing?** Give the file and section.
2. **Why?** Explain how the current wording could confuse or exclude readers.
3. **What should replace it?** Supply concrete text or a focused diff.
4. **What supports the change?** For factual or legal points, link a reliable primary source whenever possible.
5. **What could be lost in translation?** Explain significant idiomatic choices for non-English content.

Example:

> **File:** `website-copy.md`  
> **Issue:** "free support" could be read as forbidding users to ask for help.  
> **Suggestion:** Clarify that asking is welcome, but a response is not guaranteed.  
> **Reason:** Keeps the distinction between a request and an entitlement.

Please don't send unsolicited, large rewrites without first discussing their purpose.

## Translation standards

English is the primary editorial reference. The Italian version is part of the initial material; other languages are welcome when they can be reviewed properly.

- Translate the **meaning**, not just the words. Preserve the direct, human tone.
- Preserve the distinction between **legal MIT conditions**, **ethical recommendations**, and **separate support commitments**.
- Do not imply that users must pay, donate, credit the founder in their README, publish changes, or contribute code to use MIT-licensed software.
- Keep the independent-initiative disclaimer and the founder attribution accurate.
- Use terminology familiar to developers in the target language; flag uncertain legal terms.
- State whether the translation has been reviewed by a fluent speaker. A suggested translation is not automatically an approved official version.

The maintainer may request revisions, hold a translation pending review, or decline publication if its meaning cannot be verified.

## Badge and adoption boundaries

- Adoption of the manifesto is **voluntary and reversible**.
- The badge is **not** a license indicator, legal certification, or endorsement by MIT.
- Never insert new clauses into another project's MIT License text.
- Do not open unsolicited mass issues or pull requests just to add our badge to other repositories.
- Do not make adoption conditional on signing up, donating, following the founder, or linking to his personal site.
- Respect the independence of projects that disagree with the manifesto.

See [adoption-kit.md](adoption-kit.md) for approved copy-and-paste material.

## Community conduct

Discuss **ideas and behaviors**, not people's worth. Good-faith criticism of this manifesto is welcome, including strong disagreement.

Not acceptable:

- Harassment, hate, threats, personal attacks, doxxing, or targeted public shaming.
- Coordinated pressure against a project or an individual who does not adopt the manifesto.
- Spam, misleading endorsement claims, impersonation, or fabricated testimonials.
- Repeated demands for free labor, immediate replies, or personal consulting.
- Third-party content submitted without the rights needed to redistribute it.

Project maintainers may edit or close off-topic discussions, refuse contributions, and restrict participation when needed to keep the community safe and constructive. Civil disagreement is **not** grounds for moderation by itself.

## Copyright and contribution permissions

Please review [LICENSE](LICENSE) **before submitting**.

- You must have the right to submit the work, and identify any third-party material and its license.
- By submitting original **editorial text or translations** for inclusion, you agree that your contribution may be distributed and adapted under **CC BY 4.0**, while retaining your own copyright.
- By submitting original **badge artwork or copy-and-paste adoption snippets** explicitly intended for the adoption kit, you agree that those contributions may be offered under **CC0 1.0**, to the extent you hold the relevant rights.
- If you cannot accept these terms, discuss an alternative **before** submitting; do not assume the project can merge incompatible material.
- Contributors do not transfer copyright ownership to the founder simply by contributing.
- No third-party trademarks, private data, or assets should be submitted without proper authorization.

Software code is outside the editorial-content license described above; if code contributions are later accepted, their licensing terms will be stated separately before merging them.

## How editorial decisions are made

Initially, **Antonio Trento** is the founder and editorial maintainer. He makes final decisions about official wording, approved translations, and badge identity, with input from contributors.

The manifesto's core meaning should stay stable. Major changes to its principles deserve public explanation; typo fixes and clarifications do not require a referendum. No contribution, issue, or PR guarantees a merge or a response by a specific deadline.
