The MIT License is permissive on purpose. You can build, modify and sell under its terms. This page is about what happens when the code becomes part of your workflow and you need something from its maintainers.

## Before asking

Check the docs, search existing issues and try to make the problem reproducible. Tell the maintainer which version you use and what you tried. A clear report is a contribution in itself.

## When you need a commitment

If your product depends on a response by Friday, a public issue is not a purchase order. Look for a support offering, fund the work, hire someone to implement the change or maintain a fork. Your operational dependency deserves an operational plan.

## Ways to return value

- Fix a bug and offer a pull request.
- Improve documentation or tests.
- Sponsor maintenance where possible.
- Credit the project publicly, beyond the legal notices, when it makes sense.
- Tell your team why a dependency's maintainers are not its unpaid support staff.

Every item above is voluntary. None is a hidden MIT condition.

## For engineering leaders

Inventory critical dependencies. Decide which need internal ownership, a paid support path or a contingency plan. Build time for upstream contributions into the work, instead of treating them as side tasks that happen after delivery.
