The MIT License is intentionally permissive. That freedom is worth protecting. This project began with a recurring mismatch: some people accept the freedom to use software, then assume the author must also provide unlimited free support and maintenance.

The answer is to improve expectations around the license. **The MIT License — Maintainer Manifesto** is an independent, voluntary statement about clear boundaries, useful contributions and respect for maintainers.

## Who created it?

**Antonio Trento** created this initiative. You can learn about his work at [antoniotrento.net](https://antoniotrento.net). The project's value should stand on its own: no one needs to promote the founder to use or share the manifesto.

## Independence and accuracy

The project is not affiliated with or endorsed by the Massachusetts Institute of Technology, the Open Source Initiative or GitHub. Its name identifies the MIT License as the subject of discussion. It is not an official source of license text or legal advice.

We do not claim that MIT requires donations, public attribution beyond its notice requirement, or shared improvements. We do not count a repository as an adopter merely because it uses MIT.

## How to help

Challenge unclear language, propose better examples, review translations or suggest a way to make the adoption kit more useful. Use the [GitHub repository](https://github.com/themitlicense/themitlicense.github.io) for contributions. Please keep the distinction between legal conditions and voluntary principles intact.
