A good issue saves time on both sides. It can also make a project better for the next person who hits the same problem.

## Before you open it

1. Read the documentation and release notes. Search issues and discussions for the same symptom.
2. Check whether the problem reproduces on a supported version.
3. Isolate the smallest example that still fails. Remove secrets and private data.
4. Write down what you expected and what actually happened.

## A useful report in six lines

**Environment:** project version, runtime, operating system and relevant configuration.  
**Steps:** what someone else can do to reproduce the problem.  
**Expected:** the result you thought you would get.  
**Actual:** the result you received, including the exact error.  
**What I tried:** tests or workarounds already attempted.  
**Impact:** why the problem matters, without inventing a deadline for the maintainer.

## When it is urgent for you

Urgency in your business does not automatically become urgency for a volunteer. If you need a guaranteed fix date, ask whether professional support exists, budget for an engineer, or develop a patch in your own fork. The license permits those paths.

## After the answer

A maintainer may ask for more detail, decline the feature, close the issue or be unavailable. You can still contribute a fix, document a workaround or use another project. Keep the disagreement about the work, not the person.
