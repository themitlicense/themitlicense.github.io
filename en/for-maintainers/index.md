Maintaining open source can be generous and sustainable at the same time. People need to know what you can offer, how to ask and where your availability ends.

## If you maintain a small project

Put a short support note near the top of the README. Say whether issues are welcome, what information makes them useful and whether you can promise a response. You do not need a legal sounding policy to say you are working on this in your spare time.

## If your project has a growing user base

Create issue templates with reproduction steps. Document supported versions and known limits. Mark feature requests as proposals, not commitments. A public roadmap can help, but it should not imply dates you cannot guarantee.

## If companies depend on your work

Commercial use is part of the MIT bargain. You can still invite sponsorship, paid consulting or code contributions. Explain how those channels work. If you already have support contracts, honor them; this manifesto does not erase separate promises.

## A boundary you can paste today

> This project is licensed under MIT. Issues and contributions are welcome, but support, maintenance and response times are not guaranteed. Please include a clear reproduction when reporting a bug.

For a linked badge, a neutral statement and a version for non-MIT projects, use the [adoption kit](/en/adopt/).

**The goal is clarity, not a wall.** Useful reports and respectful collaboration remain welcome.
