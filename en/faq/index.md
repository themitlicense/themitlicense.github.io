<section class="page-hero"><div class="wrap"><div class="eyebrow">LICENSE VS. EXPECTATIONS</div><h1>Questions worth answering</h1><p>What MIT actually requires, what this manifesto asks, and where the boundary sits.</p></div></section>
<section class="content" markdown="1"><div class="wrap" markdown="1"><div class="prose" markdown="1">
<div class="faq"><details><summary>Is this the official MIT License website?</summary><p>No. This is an independent manifesto founded by Antonio Trento. It is not affiliated with or endorsed by the Massachusetts Institute of Technology. Read the actual license at the <a href="https://opensource.org/license/mit">Open Source Initiative</a>.</p></details><details><summary>Does the badge change my MIT License?</summary><p>No. It expresses an ethical position only. Put it in a README, never as a new clause in the `LICENSE` file.</p></details><details><summary>Does MIT require me to contribute back?</summary><p>No. MIT permits use, modification and distribution, including commercial use, subject to its terms. Sharing fixes or documentation is encouraged here, never required.</p></details><details><summary>What credit is legally required?</summary><p>The MIT License requires preserving copyright and permission notices in copies or substantial portions. Public acknowledgment on a product page is a separate courtesy.</p></details><details><summary>Can a company use MIT code commercially?</summary><p>Yes. Commercial use is allowed. If the project creates value for a business, contribution or paid support may be constructive choices, not license conditions.</p></details><details><summary>Can I ask a maintainer for help?</summary><p>Yes. A respectful question is welcome. The license itself does not promise support, response times, custom work or maintenance. A separate agreement may create its own commitments.</p></details><details><summary>Are bug reports a burden?</summary><p>Useful reports help projects. Search existing issues, include a minimal reproduction, state versions and describe what you expected. Avoid urgency claims unless someone accepted that obligation.</p></details><details><summary>Can I remove the badge?</summary><p>At any time. No account, pledge or approval is involved.</p></details><details><summary>Is the project anti-user or anti-business?</summary><p>No. The problem is treating permission to use code as a claim on a person’s unpaid time. That can come from anyone.</p></details><details><summary>Are adopters certified or verified?</summary><p>No certification exists. A badge is a self-declared expression of support, not an audit of a repository.</p></details><details><summary>Can I translate the manifesto?</summary><p>Translation proposals are welcome. Please preserve the distinction between the MIT License and voluntary principles; check the repository license for reuse terms.</p></details></div>

Still unsure? [Read the manifesto](/en/manifesto/) or [inspect the actual MIT License](https://opensource.org/license/mit).
