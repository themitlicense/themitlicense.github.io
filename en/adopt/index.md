**Keep the license. Set expectations.** Pick the format that fits your project. No signup, permission, payment or certification is involved.

## 1. Choose your signal

For MIT-licensed repositories, place the badge alongside other project information in your README. It points to the manifesto and does not replace your normal `License: MIT` badge.

## 2. Or choose words instead

A short statement explains the boundary to someone who never clicks a badge.
<div class="snippet"><div class="snippet-head"><strong>Short README statement</strong><button class="copy" type="button" data-copy="statement-en" data-label="Copy Markdown" data-done="Copied. Add it whenever you are ready." data-fallback="Select and copy">Copy Markdown</button></div><pre><code id="statement-en">### Open-source expectations

This project is available under the license stated in this repository. The
license does not by itself include guaranteed support, maintenance,
customization, or consulting. Please respect the maintainers&#x27; time.
Constructive bug reports, contributions, and acknowledgments are welcome.

We support the independent [Free Code. Not Free Labor. manifesto](https://themitlicense.github.io/en/manifesto/).</code></pre></div>
For a team with existing service commitments, the neutral version makes room for separate agreements.

## 3. Put it in the right place

Add the snippet to the README near your support or contributing section. Keep your existing `LICENSE` file and copyright notices. If you provide paid support or have a service agreement, describe that separately and precisely.

**Adoption means you support an idea.** You are free to remove the link at any time. The badge does not certify your project, change users' MIT rights or require anyone to contribute.

<div class="note">The MIT badge is for MIT-licensed repositories. For other repositories, choose the general badge or a plain statement.</div>
