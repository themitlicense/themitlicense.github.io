AN INDEPENDENT OPEN-SOURCE MANIFESTO

FREE CODE.
NOT FREE LABOR.

You can use the code. You can't claim the developer's time.

The MIT License lets you use, modify, distribute and sell software. That freedom doesn't include free consulting, urgent fixes or guaranteed support from the person who wrote it.

THE MISUNDERSTANDING

The code was shared.
The maintainer wasn't hired.

An issue is a request, not a support contract. A pull request is a contribution, not an order. You may ask. Maintainers may say no. Both things can be true.

01 / USE

Use it freely.

Fork it, ship it, build a business on it. Keep the notices required by the license.

02 / ASK

Ask with context.

Search first. Explain what broke, how to reproduce it and what you tried.

03 / RESPECT

Respect the answer.

No response time or work is owed unless someone has separately agreed to provide it.

THE PRINCIPLE

The license gives you permission. It does not give you a personal developer.
